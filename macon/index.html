Common Water Damage Issues in Macon

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. These are the ones Macon property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. For roof leaks in Macon, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. Short downspouts, soil that slopes toward the house and blocked footing drains are frequent causes. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. The team at Savannah Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

Drying Out a Macon Property: What to Expect

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Macon property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

We take moisture readings every day and compare them with a dry part of the same building. Most homes in Macon reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. The equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not when the calendar does.
