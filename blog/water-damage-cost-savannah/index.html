How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Savannah?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Savannah?

Typical Cost at a Glance

In Savannah, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. At the other end, heavy flooding or sewage with reconstruction work can go well beyond $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. Local labor rates and material prices in Savannah, GA move the numbers up or down. For a precise figure, there is no substitute for moisture readings taken during an on-site visit.

Cost by Water Category

The first thing a restoration estimate looks at is what kind of water it is. Contamination level places every loss in one of three industry categories. The dirtier the water, the more protective gear, cleaning and material removal the job needs.

Nationally, the category puts cleanup at about $3 to $7.50 for each square foot. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterSupply lines, faucets, water heater tanksRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Failed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterStorm surge, flooding rivers, sewageRoughly $7 to $7.50

The category is not fixed. Clean water that sits for a couple of days picks up bacteria and is then treated as gray or black water. It is part of why delay makes a job more expensive.

What Affects the Cost Most

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. These are the items a contractor weighs in an estimate.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Jobs with clean water cost the least. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. Given time, water works its way into wall cavities and subfloors. A leak that runs all weekend while you are gone costs far more than one caught in an hour.
  • Materials. Concrete and tile are easy to dry. Special drying or outright replacement is common for drywall, hardwood, insulation and carpet pad.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Afterward comes a second cost for new flooring, drywall, paint and cabinets, which on larger jobs often exceeds the first.

An estimate normally has two main parts, with water extraction coming before structural drying. Plan on 3 to 5 days for the drying in most cases.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are often billed by the day. Three days of drying is cheaper than six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

For the kinds of losses that come up most frequently in Savannah, national figures usually look like this. They are a place to begin, and an actual price will differ.

  • Burst pipe. Often $1,500 to $5,000 for cleanup and drying, plus the burst pipe repair itself. The total climbs when water flowed for hours or reached a second floor.
  • Appliance leak. Catch a leaking water heater, washing machine or dishwasher early and the bill is often $1,000 to $4,000. When a slow drip goes unnoticed below cabinets, concealed damage raises the price.
  • Basement flood. Typically $2,000 to $10,000 or more, depending on depth, the water source and whether the space is finished. Basement water removal in a finished basement sits at the high end.
  • Sewage backup. A common range is $3,000 to $10,000 or higher. Because sewage cleanup falls under Category 3, crews disinfect the whole area and take out porous materials.
  • Roof leak. Interior work on wet ceilings, walls and insulation often comes to $1,000 to $5,000, and the roof fix is extra.

Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage in Savannah?

The answer turns on your policy and on what caused the damage. A typical homeowners policy often covers accidental events that happen suddenly, for example a pipe bursting or a supply line giving out with no warning.

Coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. If a fitting dripped for months, an insurer may call it a maintenance issue. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. Sewer backup often needs its own add-on.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. Remember to consider your deductible. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

Ways to Lower the Cost

What happens early, within the first couple of days, determines the bulk of a water damage bill. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Each additional hour soaks more of the building.
  • Shut off the water. The moment you discover a leak, close the main shutoff, which means knowing in advance where to find it.
  • Document everything. Before moving or discarding anything, record it in photos and on video. Hold on to all receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Stay out of standing water while the electricity is on, and keep away from water that might have sewage in it. Saving money is never worth an infection or an electric shock. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

How to Get an Exact Price

National ranges are useful for planning, but your home is not an average. To learn the true spread of the water, and the work required to dry and fix it, a technician has to inspect with a meter.
