Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Savannah

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Do not think of it as clean water. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • How deep the water got and how much of the basement it covered.
  • How long the water stood before pumping and drying could begin.
  • Whether it is Category 2 or Category 3 water, since that decides what gets thrown out and disinfected.
  • If there are finished walls and floors, the tear-out of drywall, trim and carpet.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
  • A power outage during the storm with no battery backup on the pump.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Low Electrical Outlets

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Stored Belongings

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.

Carpet and Flooring

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Verify

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.
