Crawl Space Water Removal: What Savannah Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Pumping is just half of the work. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Termites and Other Pests

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Small puddles of clean water are within reach of a shop vac. Joists and subfloor stay wet, though, since a box fan only pushes moist air in circles. Crawl spaces are also tight, dark and may have live wiring or sewage in the water. Professional pumping and drying is the safer choice once the water is more than a thin puddle.
Mold can begin on damp wood and paper faced insulation within 24 to 48 hours. Little air moves down there and no sun reaches it, so natural drying is very slow. The sooner the water is out and the air is dry, the less mold has to work with. Framing that already shows growth is cleaned and then treated with antimicrobial by our crew.
The fix follows from the source of the water. Leaking pipes have to be repaired, and water from rain or the ground is typically handled with improved exterior drainage. Gutters kept clear, downspout extensions and ground that slopes off from the home each reduce the load. After drying, we tell you what we found and what would keep the space dry.
Going in while water is pooled is a risk we advise against. Many homes have electrical lines in that space, so the water itself may be live. Add the chance of contamination from drains or soil, limited room to move and bad air quality. Shine a light in from outside the opening if you like, and leave the rest to a trained crew.
