Kitchen Water Damage: What Savannah Homeowners Should Know

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Savannah homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Under the Base Cabinets

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Sink Drain Leaks

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
